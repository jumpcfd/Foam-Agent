## 1. (Note) Vortex-centre "location match" mixes a real, reproducible number with an unsourced reference value and a fragile method

**Severity:** Note — does not qualify the flow solution itself, which is independently confirmed as physically correct by boundedness and the Ghia profile comparison (finding 3 below). This concerns only the precision of one secondary physical-consistency claim.

**Evidence:** review-2.md claims a primary-vortex centre at (0.6133, 0.7383) found by minimising |U| over x,y ∈ [0.3, 0.9], "within half a grid cell" of a Ghia et al. value of (0.6172, 0.7344) that review-2.md itself flags as "from memory... indicative only." I reran the same search independently (`review-work/3/script-13.py`) and reproduced (0.61328, 0.73828) exactly. But I also ran the search *without* the hand-picked box, over the whole interior with only a thin 0.05 wall margin (`review-work/3/script-12.py`): it converges to (0.9414, 0.0586) — inside the bottom-right corner eddy, not the primary vortex — because |U| decays geometrically into the Moffatt corner eddies and is smaller there than at the true vortex core. The method only finds the primary vortex because the search box was chosen knowing where to look; it is not a self-validating localisation. I also searched the web this session (WebSearch, 2026-08-07) for a primary source giving Ghia's tabulated vortex-centre coordinates and could not retrieve one — the number in circulation cannot be pinned to an opened document any more now than it could in review-2.

**How to settle it:** locate the vortex centre by a method that doesn't require pre-selecting the search region — e.g. the extremum of a computed streamfunction (integrate U to get ψ, then find the interior stationary point) — and cite Ghia et al. (1982) Table III (vortex centres and extremal streamfunction values) directly rather than from memory. This is a nice-to-have, not a required fix: it doesn't change the digit-by-digit centreline comparison, which is the metric the request actually asked for.

## 2. (Note) spec.md's rationale for the 128×128 mesh overstates its similarity to Ghia's grid

**Severity:** Note — the underlying grid-independence conclusion is sound and separately verified (finding 3); this is an imprecision in the documented *reasoning*, not in the computation.

**Evidence:** spec.md:158-159 gives as one reason for choosing 128×128 that "128x128 is close to the 129x129 grid used in the Ghia et al. (1982) reference itself, so using a comparable resolution avoids a resolution mismatch." This case's mesh is uniform (`blockMeshDict`: `simpleGrading (1 1 1)`). Ghia et al.'s solution used a non-uniform, wall-clustered grid (the coordinate-stretching transformation described in their 1982 paper); a web search this session (2026-08-07) for the exact stretching parameters was inconclusive — one secondary source suggested Ghia's finer cases used 257×257, another the commonly-cited 129×129 for Re≤1000 — so I can't give a fully sourced correction, but "same cell count" does not mean "same near-wall resolution" between a uniform and a clustered grid, and the claim as written implies more equivalence than is likely true.

**How to settle it:** this doesn't need settling for the result to stand — the actual grid-independence claim is established directly (finding 3), not via resolution-matching to Ghia. If precision matters, soften spec.md's wording or drop the comparability argument and rely solely on the demonstrated grid-convergence.

## 3. Checks that passed (independently recomputed, not taken on trust)

- **Spec conformance.** Re-read `system/{controlDict,fvSolution,fvSchemes,blockMeshDict}`, `constant/{physicalProperties,momentumTransport}`, `0/{U,p}`, `constant/polyMesh/boundary` directly: `simpleFoam`, 128×128×1 uniform unit cube, `movingWall` = `fixedValue (1 0 0)`, `fixedWalls` = `noSlip`, `frontAndBack` = `empty` on both U and p, `nu = 0.01`, `simulationType laminar`, `residualControl` p/U at 1e-6, `pRefCell 0`/`pRefValue 0`. All match spec.md; no transcription drift, no BC on the wrong patch.

- **Convergence, independently parsed from `log.simpleFoam`** (`review-work/3/script-1.py`): 3810 `Solving for {Ux,Uy,p}` records, terminating exactly at the logged "SIMPLE solution converged in 3810 iterations." Final initial residuals: Ux = 8.40e-7, Uy = 1.00e-6, p = 6.79e-7 — all at/below the 1e-6 target. No FATAL/WARNING/Bounding lines anywhere in the log. Residual history is essentially monotonic (only 4 minor upticks over 3810×3 records, all in early transient). Beyond the residual metric itself, I checked that the *physical field* actually plateaus, by differencing the sampled centreline profile between successive `writeInterval` snapshots (`review-work/3/script-10.py`): max|Δu| falls from 0.076 (t=500→1000) to 0.00026 (t=3500→3810), a smooth, monotone decay to a flat profile — this corroborates that convergence is a real steady state, not an artifact of how the residual happens to be scaled.

- **Mass conservation**, recomputed from `3810/phi` and `constant/polyMesh/{owner,neighbour}` connectivity (`review-work/3/script-4.py`): max cell-continuity imbalance 1.30e-8 m³/s, RMS 3.36e-9 m³/s, against a characteristic face flux of 7.81e-3 m³/s (relative ≈1.7e-6). Also confirmed directly from the field file that `phi` is exactly `uniform 0` on both `movingWall` and `fixedWalls` (impermeability correctly enforced) and empty on `frontAndBack`. This matches review-2.md's number to the reported digit.

- **Momentum balance**, read directly from `postProcessing/wallForces/0/forces.dat` (binary-looking file, read with `open(...,'rb')`, `review-work/3/script-5.py`): pressure force (2.160908e-01, 3.068928e-02, 0), viscous force (−2.160915e-01, −3.068879e-02, 0), sum Fx = −7e-7, Fy = 4.9e-7 against a dominant term of ≈0.216 — matches response-2.md's claim exactly.

- **Discretisation / grid convergence**, recomputed from the archived raw data in `gridStudy/{32x32,64x64,128x128}/` on a common 201-point grid (`review-work/3/script-7.py`): 32→64: max|Δu|=0.00373, max|Δv|=0.00517; 64→128: max|Δu|=0.00091, max|Δv|=0.00094 — reproduces spec.md's addendum table exactly, confirming the archived data (added in response to review-2) is genuine and the grid study is reproducible from source, closing review-2's open item. I additionally ran a **Richardson extrapolation** (assuming 2nd-order convergence, consistent with the ~4–5.5× error reduction observed on grid halving) to estimate the zero-grid-spacing limit and compared both the 128×128 result and the extrapolated limit against Ghia (`review-work/3/script-9.py`): the extrapolated profile is essentially identical to the 128×128 profile and is *not* systematically closer to Ghia (e.g. at y=0.9531, |u₁₂₈−Ghia|=0.00372 vs |u_extrap−Ghia|=0.00385). This is a stronger, arithmetic version of review-2's qualitative argument: it shows the small residual disagreement with Ghia is not a mesh-resolution artifact of this case, since refining further (even to the infinite-resolution limit) doesn't reduce it.

- **Field boundedness / 2D consistency**, read directly from `3810/{U,p}` (`review-work/3/script-11.py`): Ux ∈ [−0.243, 0.976], Uy ∈ [−0.534, 0.315], p ∈ [−3.02, 3.95], no NaN/Inf anywhere, Uz exactly 0.0 in every one of 16384 cells (genuine 2D solution, not just declared as such). No convective overshoot above the 1 m/s lid speed.

- **Comparison with Ghia, Ghia & Shin (1982) Table I**, recomputed independently by linear interpolation of `postProcessing/centerlineSampling/3810/{vertical,horizontal}Centerline.xy` onto Ghia's 17 tabulated points (`review-work/3/script-8.py`). Reference values re-fetched this session via WebFetch from [gist: u-velocity](https://gist.github.com/ivan-pi/3e9326d18a366ffe6a8e5bfda6353219) and [gist: v-velocity](https://gist.github.com/ivan-pi/caa6c6737d36a9140fbcf2ea59c78b3c), retrieved 2026-08-07 (the same source review-2 used; I re-pulled it independently rather than trusting the earlier transcription, and it also matches the commonly-published table from memory, which is corroborating but not a second independent primary source). Result: u(y) max|diff| = 0.00491 (0.49% of lid speed), RMS = 0.00225; v(x) max|diff| = 0.00878 (0.88%), RMS = 0.00444 — matching review-2.md's numbers exactly. Diffs are small and one-signed by region (not scattered noise), consistent with the Richardson-extrapolation finding above that this is a solver/discretisation difference between this case and Ghia's 1982 method rather than under-resolution here.

## What remains unchecked

- No comparison against a second literature dataset (e.g. Botella & Peyret's spectral benchmark) has been made, by either this or prior reviews.
- No experimental data exists for this configuration; every comparison here, in all three reviews, is solver-vs-solver against a 1982 finite-difference/multigrid solution, not solver-vs-experiment.
- The exact primary-vortex-centre value attributed to Ghia et al. (1982) could not be sourced from a primary document this session (see finding 1) — treat any such comparison, in this review or prior ones, as indicative only.
- Ghia et al.'s actual grid topology (uniform vs. stretched, exact point count) was not conclusively confirmed from a primary source this session (finding 2); this affects only a documentation remark, not the computed result.

**Overall assessment:** the case is sound. Both defects raised in review-1 and review-2 were genuinely fixed, not just asserted fixed — I confirmed this by recomputing from the same source files response-2.md points to, and got the same numbers. The two items I'm adding are notes on the precision of secondary, non-load-bearing claims, not on the CFD result, which converges cleanly, conserves mass and momentum to solver precision, and matches the standard published benchmark to well under 1% of the lid speed.
